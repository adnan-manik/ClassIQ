Problem Statement

Every semester, Heads of Department assign teachers to class sections through a largely manual, intuition-driven process — with no structured way to evaluate teacher–student compatibility before the term begins. The result:

  • !
    Style mismatches between how teachers teach and how students prefer to learn, hurting engagement and outcomes.
  • !
    Problems are only discovered weeks into the semester, when it is far too late to course-correct.
  • !
    Peer relationships — friendships, conflicts, study partnerships — are never considered during section planning, despite strongly shaping classroom dynamics.
The gap

Tools like Canvas, Blackboard and Google Classroom only track what happened. No platform predicts what is likely to happen before a semester even starts.

Our Approach

ClassIQ builds digital twins of every student and teacher — structured behavioral profiles from psychological survey data — and simulates how a proposed classroom would perform before the semester begins. It is a decision-support tool, not a guarantee engine: HoDs test configurations, compare predicted outcomes side-by-side, and assign with evidence.

Digital Twins

Students & teachers replicated as data profiles to test scenarios safely.

Learning Analytics

Felder–Silverman learning-style profiling across four simple axes.

Social Network Analysis

Peer ties modeled as a weighted graph — cohesion, conflict, isolation.

Agent-Based Simulation

What-if section configurations scored & compared before they go live.

Key differentiator

The first platform to combine learning-style profiling, peer social-graph analysis, and what-if simulation for pre-semester classroom configuration.

Conclusion

ClassIQ turns semester planning from intuition into evidence. By building digital twins from psychological survey data, modeling peer relationships as a social graph, and scoring proposed sections through a transparent four-factor engine, it lets HoDs see how a classroom will perform before it exists — and choose the configuration most likely to succeed. The core engine, social-graph analysis, section builder, scenario comparison and reporting are already built and working, ahead of the original FYP schedule.
